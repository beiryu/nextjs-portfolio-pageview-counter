## Technology Stack

Next.js (App Router), TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, React Server Components, Auth.js, Prisma, PostgreSQL, Cloudinary, Google Maps API, Vercel

## Overview

Duration: 7/2024 - ongoing

Vietnam is moving from petrol to electric faster than its charging information is keeping up. Drivers can find a station on a map and still arrive to find it occupied, broken, or the wrong connector. That gap — between a station existing and a station being usable right now — is the problem this platform addresses.

The MVP is deliberately narrow: **find a working charger, and understand what running an EV actually costs you.** Everything else is deferred until those two are solid, starting in Ho Chi Minh City rather than attempting national coverage with thin data.

## Charging Station Map

- **Interactive map** of stations near the driver's location, built on Google Maps so navigation hands off to a tool people already have
- **Availability status** — how many ports are free, not just that the station exists
- **Station detail** — charging speed, connector types, operating hours, and pricing, which is the information that decides whether a driver bothers going
- **Community-contributed data** — drivers add and correct stations, with a validation step before contributions go live, because official data alone will never be complete or current enough
- **Fault reporting** — a broken unit flagged by one driver saves every driver behind them
- **Favourites** for the stations a driver actually uses

## Cost Calculator

The single strongest conversion tool for EV adoption is arithmetic, and most prospective buyers never do it:

- **Petrol versus electricity comparison** based on the driver's own daily distance, vehicle, and local fuel price
- **Full cost breakdown** — fuel, maintenance, and tax differences, not just the cost per kilometre
- **Home charging optimisation** — local time-of-use electricity rates entered once, then the cheapest charging windows identified, with warnings for peak-rate periods
- **Long-term projection** — cumulative savings visualised over time, which is where the argument actually becomes persuasive

## Community Layer

- **Question and answer forum** organised by topic — charging, maintenance, vehicle comparison, troubleshooting
- **Guides and tutorials** for people considering their first EV
- **Contributor recognition** through a badge system, since a crowdsourced data platform lives or dies on whether contributing feels worthwhile
- **Vietnam-specific guidance** from local owners, which is the part no international EV resource provides

## Technical Implementation

- **Server Components** for the map shell and station pages, keeping initial load fast on mobile connections
- **PostgreSQL with Prisma** modelling stations, ports, connector types, and availability history
- **A contribution pipeline** with validation and moderation before user-submitted data reaches the map
- **Geospatial querying** for proximity search
- **Caching on read-heavy paths** to keep map interaction responsive and API usage bounded
- **Bilingual interface** — Vietnamese first, English available
- **Offline access** to saved favourites and recently viewed stations, for the parts of a journey where connectivity is unreliable

This project is about building for a market in transition: identifying the two questions that actually block adoption, solving those completely, and designing the data layer so the community can fill in what no official source will.
